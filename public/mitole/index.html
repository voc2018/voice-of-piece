The artist

In his own words

“I hope that people can experience many things through my art and my paintings. When they look at my work, I hope they find the good rather than the bad, and come away with positive thoughts. And I hope my work can be a good influence on Tanzanian society — and beyond it, on Africa as a whole.”

“Natumaini watu wataweza kupata uzoefu wa mambo mengi kupitia sanaa yangu na michoro yangu. Wanapotazama kazi zangu, natumaini wataona mema kuliko mabaya, na kuondoka na mawazo chanya.”

“제 그림을 통해 사람들이 많은 것을 경험하기를 바랍니다. 제 작품을 볼 때 나쁜 것보다 좋은 것을 보고, 긍정적인 생각을 안고 돌아가기를 바랍니다. 그리고 제 작품이 탄자니아 사회, 나아가 아프리카 전체에 좋은 영향을 주기를 바랍니다.”

The Tingatinga tradition

From one animal to a whole city

Mitole works within the Tingatinga school — the movement of bold enamel colour and black outline founded in Dar es Salaam by Edward Saidi Tingatinga in 1968 — and has extended it into territory entirely his own. Where the tradition typically isolates a single animal against flat colour, Mitole composes panoramas of civic life: the primary school, the fruit market, the national hospital, the railway to Moshi.

See the gallery

Line first, colour after

Each work begins as weeks of patient black-line drawing before any colour is laid in. Hand-lettered Swahili signs — Shule ya Msingi, Soko la Matunda, Twende Tukasome! — anchor every scene in a real, working Tanzania, observed with humour and tenderness. His paintings are held by collectors internationally. Through Voice of Piece, they travel directly from his studio in Dar es Salaam to homes and galleries worldwide.
